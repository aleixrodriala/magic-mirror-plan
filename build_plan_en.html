How It All Fits — Technical Drawings & Wiring

Exact millimeter dimensions from design.py · Zero 230V mains inside the spruce frame

Front elevation (what is hidden behind the glass) 1242 × 942 mm
Scaled joint drawing: sections, rear view and every screw dimensions in mm · 69 + 16 = 85 to the wall

The whole mirror is two blocks: the frame, which carries the glass, and the backplate, which carries the monitor and the hanging cleat. The two blocks are joined by 22 sideways screws, no glue, so it can be opened again.

  • The glass is not screwed anywhere. It rests on the bottom rail and is trapped between the lip (front) and the glass stops and reinforcement (back).
  • No screw reaches the glass. They go in sideways, from below or from the back. Only the glass-stop screws (C) point towards the glass, which is why they must not exceed 35 mm.
  • The weight goes up through the backplate. Frame → inner cleats → backplate → 45° cleat → wall.

Why plywood in two thicknesses — and how the corners work

  • Frame corners — steel brackets, NOT plywood: The 4 mitered spruce corners are held by glue, a strap clamp, and 4 steel L-brackets (40 × 40 × 15 mm) screwed inside the back corners.
  • 9 mm backplate (1204 × 904 mm): Keeps the whole frame square, carries the monitor (via VESA straps), holds the Pi/ESP32, and mounts the hanging cleat. 9 mm is stiff enough without adding unnecessary weight (~4.2 kg instead of ~7.0 kg in 15 mm).
  • 15 mm bottom shelf (1200 × 83 mm): Stands upright behind the bottom edge of the glass, screwed up through the bottom spruce rail. Standing 83 mm tall on edge acts like a beam so the 16.2 kg glass cannot bow the spruce rail; 15 mm gives enough edge meat to take the 6× 4×40 screws cleanly without splitting.
  • 15 mm 45° hanging cleat (900 × 145 mm ripped at 45° → 2 halves): One half bolts to the wall, the other mounts on the mirror; gravity locks them together. 15 mm gives a strong bevel hook for ≈ 30 kg without splitting around screws.
  • 18 mm spacer under the mirror cleat (900 × 69 mm — spruce strip, not plywood): A straight length of the same 18 × 69 mm frame spruce glued and screwed behind the backplate under the mirror cleat. It stands the mirror off the wall so the monitor plugs have 27 mm clearance and the frame clears the drywall by 16 mm.

Stock sizes are Leroy Merlin's: planed spruce (listón de abeto cepillado) 18 × 69 mm and 17 × 17 mm, read on their site on 26 Nov. The whole cut list depends on these two sections. Brico Depot sells an 18 × 70 mm planed pine strip that can replace the 18 × 69 for the frame rails (1 mm deeper, nothing else changes), but it has no 17 × 17 square (only 15 × 15 and 21 × 21): buy the squares at Leroy Merlin, or change SQ in design.py and rebuild the page.

Spruce stock tips & finish: Sight down every strip at the store and pick the straightest ones. Planed store stock usually has slightly rounded edges, so a fine hairline seam shows on the front face where the 17 × 17 mm lip meets the 18 × 69 mm strip — sand flush after gluing or leave it as a clean reveal. Finish: none is required on the exterior spruce (optional: 1 coat of clear matte water-based varnish, e.g. Luxens incoloro mate 250 ml, Leroy Merlin Ref. 16700152, €8.49). Keep the matte black spray paint for the inside frame cavity, shelf, stops and backplate.

Lip alternatives (not the default): Default 17 × 17 mm lip gives a 35 mm visible front face (1172 × 872 mm visible mirror, 14 mm glass overlap/side) with zero frame rips — note that 35 mm is the widest front face of the three options, so confirm you like that look before gluing.
(a) Slimmer 28 mm face: rip the 17 × 17 mm square strip to 10 × 17 mm (visible mirror 1186 × 886 mm, 7 mm overlap/side). Nothing else changes, so you can still decide at glue-up, but it adds 1 table-saw rip.
(b) Slimmer 78 mm total depth: 10 × 10 mm lip + frame strips ripped to 65 mm (78 mm front-to-wall). Requires 2 extra rips and must be decided before buying.

Already in hand at home — quick checks before ordering / assembling

  • Two-way mirror glass (1200 × 900 × 6 mm — measured, off shopping list): Check against your sheet that light transmission is ≥ 20 % (Pilkington MirroView type — classic grey spy glass gives ≈ 11 % and makes the screen look dim), that it is tempered or laminated 3+3 safety glass, and has polished edges (cantos pulidos). Also check whether rubber setting blocks came with the glass (if not, buy the 2 hard-rubber blocks + 4× 3 mm side shims in the checklist below).
  • Monitor (in hand — off shopping list): Confirm it is the Philips 27E2N1100L/00 (body 614 × 363 × 32 mm without stand, lit 598 × 336 mm). Measure its real DC barrel plug now before ordering any right-angle DC adapter, and verify its real depth before fixing the 18 mm hanging spacer.

Phase 1

Workshop (Dosrius, Fri)

Build the frame, cut all wood/plywood/straps, dry-fit with a 1200 × 900 mm template, and pre-drill every hole — the glass never leaves home.

Glue the 17 × 17 mm lips onto the 18 × 69 mm strips FIRST

Do first · 30–45 min glue cureConfirm lip width first
  • Sight down the spruce strips and pick the two straightest 18 × 69 × 2400 mm strips and two 17 × 17 × 2400 mm square strips.
  • Confirm lip width before gluing: default 17 × 17 mm gives a 35 mm visible face with zero rips (optional: rip to 10 × 17 mm for a slimmer 28 mm face right now).
  • Glue one square strip full-length flush along the front inside edge of each 2.4 m frame strip using ~8 clamps per strip (or tight masking tape + flat weights). Wipe squeeze-out cleanly; note planed edges leave a thin hairline seam on the front face (sand flush once dry or accept it).
  • Start Step 02 immediately while the lip glue cures for 30–45 min — do not cut miters until the glue sets.

Cut all plywood pieces, inner cleats, stops, spacer & VESA straps (while lips cure)

Table saw + Jigsaw + Miter saw
  • 9 mm backplate (store-cut to 1204 × 904 mm): mark and jigsaw the 644 × 378 mm monitor window (280 mm from left/right edges, 82 mm from bottom edge). Then clip all 4 corners of the backplate at 45°, 50 × 50 mm: the steel corner brackets stay in the frame, and with only 1 mm of clearance per side a square corner would not get past them.
  • 15 mm bottom shelf: table-saw rip a 1200 × 83 mm strip from the 60 × 120 cm sheet, then jigsaw the 101 × 6 mm top-edge notch for the monitor buttons (405–506 mm from the right end of the shelf).
  • 15 mm 45° French cleat: table-saw rip a 900 × 145 mm strip, tilt the blade to 45°, and rip once straight down the middle to get two matching halves (~78 mm long face / ~63 mm flat face). Ready-made fallback: if skipping the 45° rip, use ready-made aluminium French-cleat rails instead (Amazon.es, seen 26 Nov: Uniclife 61 cm, 2 pairs for €36.99, the seller claims 68 kg per pair; or a Taskar 45 cm Z-bar, €15.49, seller claims 40 kg with solid fixing, ≈ 6 mm deep). These are sellers' figures with no test standard behind them; the mirror is ≈ 30 kg, so use both 61 cm pairs side by side and still hit ≥ 2 studs. The hanging happens at home, so this does not block Friday. A rail is much thinner than 15 mm ply: measure it and make the packing under the mirror rail 33 mm minus the rail depth (e.g. an offcut of the 9 mm backplate sheet on top of the 18 mm spacer) to keep 85 mm to the wall; the two bottom stand-off blocks keep their 33 mm thickness.
  • Miter/hand cuts: cut the 4 inner cleats from 2× 17 × 17 mm strips (2× 1120 mm + 2× 820 mm — opening minus 43 mm per end to clear the 40×40 brackets), the 3 glass stops from the same 17 × 17 strip (1× 1120 mm top, 2× 780 mm sides), 2 bottom stand-off blocks (69 × 70 × 33 mm = 18 mm spruce cut from the spare strip + 15 mm ply offcut, glued together), and cut the aluminium bar into 2× 320 mm VESA straps (drill VESA 100 M4 + backplate holes). The 900 mm hanging spacer is the 0.9 m piece of 18 × 69 as bought: no cut.

Miter-cut the lipped strips & glue up the frame corners

Keep clamped ≥60–90 minOuter 1242 × 942 mm
  • Once the lip glue has cured (30–45 min), cut 45° miters through strip + lip together: each 2.4 m lipped strip yields one 1242 mm rail and one 942 mm rail (outer tip-to-tip; inner opening 1206 × 906 mm).
  • Dry-fit with a strap and the 4× printed 90° miter jigs (STL 01). The frame perimeter is 4.37 m, so the strap must be at least 5 m (a 4 m band clamp does not go round). Work flat on the bench, put a scrap of wood under the buckle, tape the printed jigs so the glue does not stick to them, and tighten only until a thin bead of glue squeezes out. A second strap (one near the front face, one near the back) keeps the 69 mm deep frame from twisting. Check both diagonals match within 1–2 mm (outer ≈ 1559 mm, inner ≈ 1508 mm).
  • Glue the 4 miters, pull tight with the strap clamp, re-check diagonals, and screw the 4× 40 × 40 × 15 mm steel corner brackets inside the rear corners (y ≈ 53–68 mm, behind backplate) with 16× 3.5×16 mm pan-head screws. They stay in for good; the backplate unit drops in past them thanks to its clipped corners and the 43 mm the stops and cleats stop short.
  • Cure & Friday time fallback: keep corners clamped at least 60–90 min (ideally 2 h) before lifting or loading in a car; the glue only reaches full strength the next day, so carry the frame flat, with the backplate screwed in, and do not hang anything on it on Friday. If Friday runs short on time, finish all machine cuts (lips glued, miters cut, plywood ripped/windowed, 45° cleat ripped) at the workshop — corner glue-up, backplate assembly, pre-drilling and painting need no workshop machines and can be finished at home.

Assemble the backplate unit (stops, inner cleats, hanging spacer & cleat)

Order matters · Max 3.5×35 into stopsMount cleat BEFORE glass
  • Inner cleats FIRST (glued for good to the backplate, NOT the frame): glue the 4× 17 × 17 mm cleats (2× 1120, 2× 820 mm) onto the REAR face of the backplate, flush with its edges and starting 42 mm from each backplate corner (= 43 mm from the frame's inner corners, the room the steel brackets need). Screw them with 22× 3.5×20 mm from the FRONT of the backplate (9 mm of ply + 11 mm into the cleat; Ø 2 mm pilot, heads countersunk flush; one every ~18 cm: 6 per long cleat, 5 per short). Do not screw from the back of the cleat into the 9 mm ply: too little thread, and the tip comes through.
  • Stops SECOND: lay the 17 × 17 mm top stop (1120 mm, centred) and the two side stops (780 mm, starting 82 mm above the backplate's bottom edge, level with the shelf top) on the FRONT of the backplate, flush with its edges, directly in front of the cleats (they cover the cleat screw heads). They stop short of the two top corners like the cleats; the bottom stays open for the 15 mm shelf. No glue. Screw them from the REAR of the cleats, through cleat and backplate, with 10× 3.5×35 mm (4 top, 3 per side; Ø 2 mm pilot, between the cleat screws): 17 + 9 + 9 mm into the stop. MAX 3.5×35: it is only 44 mm from the cleat's rear face to the glass.
  • Hanging spacer + 45° cleat (glued for good BEFORE glass goes in): measure the real monitor depth first (Philips spec is 32 mm → 27 mm clear to wall with 18 mm spacer). Glue and screw the 900 × 69 × 18 mm spruce spacer centered on the rear of the backplate (73 mm down from outer frame top = 54 mm down from backplate top edge) with ~8× 3.5×20 mm screws from the front of the backplate. Glue and screw the mirror's half of the 15 mm 45° cleat on top of the spacer (bevel on BOTTOM, sloping down toward the wall) with ~8× 3.5×30 mm screws.
  • Glue/screw the two 69 × 70 × 33 mm bottom wood stand-off blocks onto the rear of the backplate near the bottom-left and bottom-right corners (inside the inner cleats & brackets).

Dry-fit with a 1200 × 900 mm template, pre-drill every hole, spray & pack for transport

Dry-fit before leavingOuter 124.2 × 94.2 × 6.9 cm
  • Drop a 1200 × 900 mm cardboard or MDF template into the frame opening, packed around the rim to 8 mm total thickness (6 mm glass + 2 mm EPDM) plus 1 mm felt allowance. Confirm 3 mm clearance all round and equal diagonals (≈ 1508 mm inner).
  • Drop the 15 mm bottom shelf in behind the template and pre-drill 6× 4×40 mm holes straight up through the bottom rail into the center of the 15 mm ply edge (Ø 2.5 mm pilot in ply so it won't split; countersink heads). NO glue — screws only, installed at home after the glass.
  • Seat the backplate unit behind the template + shelf and pre-drill all 22× 3.5×30 mm sideways holes from the inner cleats into the 18 mm spruce frame (Ø 4 mm clearance in cleats, Ø 2 mm pilot in frame). NO glue — this is the serviceable joint! (Do NOT try to screw the backplate straight into the frame front-to-back.)
  • Also pre-drill: the 4 VESA strap holes (Ø 4.5 mm) in the 45 mm zone above the backplate monitor window, and push the 4 M4×20 bolts through from the FRONT of the backplate (tape the heads so they stay in: once the backplate is closed the front cannot be reached), 4× Ø 2 mm pilot holes at the backplate center (z = 560–616 mm) for the Pi 5 brass M2.5 standoffs (using optional STL 07 as a template), the camera ribbon slot through the backplate (20 × 4 mm, centred left-right and 49 mm above the top edge of the monitor window, between the two VESA straps and just under the Pi 5 holes: two Ø 4 mm holes joined with a jigsaw or file; see the slot detail in the joint drawing), and the anti-lift keeper screw hole under the bottom rail.
  • Finish & drying: spray the inside cavity, shelf, stops and front of backplate matte black (2 thin coats, dry ~1–2 h). Exterior spruce needs no finish; if adding 1 optional coat of clear matte water-based varnish, wait ≥4–6 h (ideally overnight) before laying the frame face-down on a blanket at home.
  • Transport home: outer size is 124.2 × 94.2 × 6.9 cm — check car boot/seats. Travel with the backplate screwed in (at least 6–8 of the sideways 3.5×30 screws) so the frame stays dead square in transit.

Bench-test monitor, radar against real glass & WLED LED strip

Bench tests firstSet 4000 mA WLED cap
  • Monitor check (already in hand): verify Philips 27E2N1100L/00 (614 × 363 × 32 mm, lit 598 × 336 mm), test the 90° micro-HDMI cable, measure the real DC barrel plug before buying an angled DC adapter, cover logo/power LED with black tape, and check ddcutil/auto-power-on.
  • Radar bench test (H3): wire the HLK-LD2410C with its 1.5 m 4-wire cable and test presence detection on a table right behind your real 1200 × 900 × 6 mm glass. If it detects cleanly through the mirror coating, mount it in STL 03 inside the cavity left of the monitor; if the coating blocks 24 GHz, mount it underneath the bottom rail.
  • WLED setup & halo test (L1): the GLEDOPTO controller comes with WLED installed: power it from the 5 V supply, join its Wi-Fi “WLED-AP” (password wled1234) and put it on the home network. Set strip type SK6812 RGBW (Warm White), LED count (~250 for ~4.16 m — match trimmed strip), and Maximum Current = 4000 mA. Wiring, no soldering: 5 V and GND from the supply into the controller's input terminals; controller output (V+, DATA, GND) to the start of the strip with under 30 cm of cable; inject 5 V + GND at the other end too, straight from the supply, via Wago 221-413 levers. The level shifter is inside the controller (if the strip flickers, then a separate 74AHCT125 is needed). Hold the lit strip behind the frame against the wall to decide final LED placement (rear face of inner cleats vs. backplate clips).

Seat the 1200 × 900 × 6 mm glass, bottom shelf & light-block mask

2 people + suction cups + gloves · 16.2 kg
  • Unscrew the transport screws, lift out the backplate, and lay the frame face-down on a clean blanket on a table. Apply 10 × 2 mm EPDM glazing tape continuously around the front spruce lip.
  • Run the pen-tip test on the glass: on the coated face the pen tip touches its own reflection → place coated side facing DOWN (toward the room).
  • With 2 people wearing gloves and using suction cups, lower the glass gently onto the EPDM lip. Then push the 2× hard-rubber setting blocks (100 × 8 × 3 mm, 80–90 Shore A) into the 3 mm gap between the glass and the bottom rail at the ¼ points (30 & 90 cm from the glass's left edge), and the 4× 3 mm lateral shims into the side gaps (2 per side).
  • Stick 1 mm black adhesive felt onto the front face of the 15 mm bottom shelf, and a 2 mm pad (two layers of the same felt) onto its rear face so the backplate can rest on it, drop it in behind the glass, and drive the 6× 4×40 mm screws up through the pre-drilled bottom rail (snug, NO glue).
  • Lay in the razor-cut matte black card overlay (640 × 390 mm, 598 × 336 mm lit window) and the 5 mm black foam-board mask (1170 × 805 mm; cut a Ø 20 mm camera aperture centred left-right, 89 mm above the top edge of the lit window, i.e. 177 cm from the floor once hung; Ø 12 mm clips the wide lens. Keep everything behind it matte black).

Close the backplate, mount monitor, centered Pi 5 & sensors

Screws only (serviceable)2 mm VESA spacers
  • Stick 1 mm black adhesive felt onto the front faces of the 17 × 17 mm stops (already screwed to the front of the backplate) and stick 15 mm black adhesive foam strips (replaces STL 04) around the front of the backplate inside the stops to hold the 5 mm foam mask flat.
  • Lower the backplate unit gently onto the glass + shelf and drive all 22× 3.5×30 mm sideways screws from the inner cleats into the 18 mm spruce frame (NO glue — snug without stressing the glass). Then cover the two small openings left at the top rear corners, where the backplate corners are clipped, with black gaffer tape so the LED light cannot leak in behind the glass.
  • Line the Philips monitor bezel with 1 mm black felt, seat it through the 644 × 378 mm backplate window, and bolt both 320 mm aluminium VESA straps to the monitor (4× M4×10 + washers) and to the backplate (slip the straps over the 4 M4×20 bolts already sticking out of the backplate and fit the nuts from the rear) with 2 mm spacers between each strap and the backplate so tightening does not push the monitor into the glass. Leave a 1 mm clearance slit under the monitor above the bottom shelf (shim 1 mm while tightening the straps, sealed behind the glass by the black card/felt against light leaks).
  • Screw the brass M2.5 standoffs into the 4 pre-drilled Ø 2 mm pilot holes at the backplate center (open-air cooling) and mount Pi 5 + Active Cooler. Mount the GLEDOPTO LED controller, pass the Pi 5 camera cable through the ribbon slot just below the camera and fold the spare length flat (install Pi Cam 3 once STL 02 is redesigned; fix its focus in software so it does not hunt on the glass), cover the slot with black tape, mount the radar (cavity or under bottom rail per Step 06 bench test) and stick USB mic + 1 m extension under the bottom rail.
  • Stick thin felt wall pads onto the rear face of the two 33 mm bottom wood stand-off blocks.

Hang on drywall (pladur) above the piano & lock anti-lift keeper

≥2 studs + 4 toggles · ≈30 kgMeasure piano lid first
  • Measure before drilling: open the piano lid and set up the music stand to confirm they clear the 20 cm gap above the 102.5 cm piano (frame 122.5–216.7 cm, screen 134.5–168.1 cm), and verify ≥15 mm ceiling/wall clearance above 216.7 cm to lift onto the cleat.
  • Wall-cleat height formula: Wall-cleat top tip = 1225 + 806 = 2031 mm (203.1 cm) from floor (= frame top 216.7 cm − 13.6 cm; straight bottom edge of wall cleat sits at 195.3 cm). Verify exact distance d on the built mirror from outer top edge to inside bevel corner, then set wall-cleat top tip at 216.7 cm − d.
  • Mount the 900 mm 45° wall cleat into drywall studs (do NOT use the TV bracket): locate ≥2 vertical pladur studs (montantes) with a magnet/stud finder, level the wall half (bevel on TOP, sloping DOWN toward the wall to form a V-pocket), and anchor into ≥2 studs with 4.8×45 mm self-drilling stud screws plus 4× Fischer DuoTec 10 or metal Molly M6 toggles (≥25 kg each).
  • Lift the mirror onto the cleat with 2 people and fit the anti-lift keeper (M4: small steel L-bracket anchored to the wall under the bottom edge with a 3.5×16 mm screw up into the pre-drilled bottom-rail hole).
  • Plug the 230 V power strip into the confirmed wall socket behind the piano and fix it at ≈ 90 cm height (~1.0 m run fits the Pi 5's 1.2 m PSU cable). Route the 3 low-voltage cables inside the white cable channel (Leroy Merlin stocks 10 × 15 mm), pair Pi 5 Bluetooth to the soundbar, run a heat test with the real monitor running, and verify WLED triggers.
